Trust and security

What this product does with data, what protects it, and — just as important — what is not done yet. Everything on this page can be checked against the tenant's own hash-chained audit log rather than taken on trust.

The short version

  • Your CRM records never reach the model. Identity resolution returns a classification and a permitted behaviour — never a record, a name, a company or a deal.
  • Consent comes before matching, in code. Absence of an affirmative event is refusal, not a default, and the exact wording shown is stored verbatim in the visitor's own language.
  • The AI disclosure cannot be switched off. It is in the surface, on the first screen, every conversation — and the coverage figure is on a scorecard you can export.
  • Ownership, lifecycle stage and pipeline are read-only. Enforced in three independent places, because overwriting a CRM owner is the one thing a RevOps buyer will not forgive.
  • A commitment made to a person is never retracted to keep a system consistent. A confirmed booking stays confirmed; the CRM is repaired behind it.

Security controls

Tenant isolation
The tenant id is derived from the API key, never accepted from the caller. Under the Postgres adapters every query runs inside a transaction that binds SET LOCAL app.tenant_id, under a NOBYPASSRLS role with a forced row-level security policy: a query that forgets the binding returns zero rows.
Credentials at rest
CRM credentials are sealed with a per-tenant data key under a KMS root key, with the tenant id authenticated as additional data — a ciphertext moved between rows does not decrypt. Rotation rewraps without the plaintext leaving the process.
Keys
Stored as SHA-256 digests, compared in constant time, shown once. They expire, record last use, and rotate with an overlap window. A public widget key is bound to your registered web origins and refused from anywhere else.
Abuse and spend
Per-key, per-IP and per-session rate limits, a hard message ceiling per conversation, an input length cap before tokenisation, and your spend cap checked before the model is called rather than reconciled afterwards.
Prompt injection and output
Retrieved content never enters the system prompt; it arrives inside a per-turn unguessable delimiter as data. Detection runs over normalised text with multilingual patterns and an optional classifier stage, and every answer — including each sentence of a streamed one — is validated before emission for invented prices, human claims and exfiltration links.
Browser and transport
A strict Content-Security-Policy with no 'unsafe-inline', frame-ancestors on the conversation panel restricted to your registered origins, HSTS, a restrictive Permissions-Policy, and HTTP timeouts.

Data residency and retention

Residency is UK or EU per tenant. A deployment declares the regions it operates; a tenant whose residency has no declared region is refused rather than quietly served from the wrong region, and a deployment that declares none makes no residency claim at all.

DataDefault retentionConfigurable
Conversation transcripts90 daysYes
Voice recordings30 days, off by defaultYes
Lead personal data730 daysYes
Audit entries7 yearsNo

Sessions expire after 30 minutes idle or 24 hours absolute, and the transcript goes with the session. Audit entries are exempt from erasure on legal-obligation grounds and hold no personal data, because payloads are redacted before they are written.

What is not done yet

Stated here rather than left to be discovered. A trust page that overstates is worse than none.

  • Administrative SSO and MFA. Administrative access is a bearer key today. This is the largest open item and belongs before a production pilot.
  • An independent penetration test. Not yet commissioned.
  • SOC 2 and ISO/IEC 27001. Not started. SOC 2 Type I is targeted within twelve months of first revenue.
  • An independent accessibility audit. The product is built to WCAG 2.2 AA and the conformance statement refuses to claim conformance without a named auditor and a date, so it reads "partial, self-assessed".
  • A public status page. Not yet published.
